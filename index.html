Where the weight sits

The page budget gives the book away. Chapter 3 alone is 104 pages — a full scikit-learn course — and Chapter 5 is 80 pages of quantum mechanics with no machine learning in it at all. Chapter 7 is ten pages. Read the ladder on the left as a bar chart and the shape is obvious.

인공지능과 화학의 만남

AI meets chemistrypp. 11–609 sections

The foundation, and it solves exactly one problem: machine learning eats vectors of numbers, but chemistry hands you molecules. Everything in this chapter is that conversion.

  • 1.1

    화학 물질의 구조와 표현 Structure & representation

    SMILES (aspirin is CC(=O)Oc1ccccc1C(=O)O), InChI/InChIKey, MOL/SDF. One molecule has many valid SMILES but exactly one InChI — which is why InChI is the database key.

    12
  • 1.2

    화학 물질 표현 인자 Descriptors

    MW, LogP, TPSA, H-bond donors/acceptors, rotatable bonds. RDKit ships ~200. Physically meaningful, which is why a forest on descriptors stays interpretable.

    13
  • 1.3

    화학에서 사용되는 Python과 구글 코랩 Python & Colab

    Environment setup. Sensible here — RDKit and PySCF installs are painful locally, and Colab gives you a free GPU for Ch 4.

    18
  • 1.4

    RDKIT과 화학정보학 RDKit & cheminformatics

    The core library. Chem.MolFromSmiles(), SMARTS substructure matching, sanitization, 2D depiction, conformers. If you take one thing from Ch 1, take this.

    24
  • 1.5

    분자 속성 시각화 Property visualization

    Drawing molecules, coloring atoms by contribution. Sets up the visualization that matters later: which substructure drove a prediction.

    32
  • 1.6

    화학 표현자 변환 Format conversion

    SMILES ↔ InChI ↔ MOL, plus canonicalization. Reads like plumbing, but duplicates hiding under different SMILES are a top cause of inflated scores.

    34
  • 1.7

    화학 지문과 표현법 Fingerprints

    The central idea of the field. Hash every substructure within radius r of each atom into bit positions → a 2048-bit vector (Morgan/ECFP). Also MACCS's 166 predefined keys. This is how a variable-size graph becomes fixed-size input.

    37
  • 1.8

    화학적 유사도 계산 Similarity

    Tanimoto: shared bits ÷ total bits set. Encodes the field's founding assumption — similar structure, similar property. Know that it has teeth: activity cliffs break it.

    46
  • 1.9

    화학 반응 예측 Reaction prediction

    Reaction SMARTS and templates: encode a transformation as a pattern, apply to reactants, get products. Rule-based retrosynthesis in miniature.

    52

화학 정보의 탐색: Chemical Space와 빅데이터

Chemical space & big datapp. 61–922 sections

Short, but it is the pivot from one molecule to millions.

  • 2.1

    화학 물질의 '화학 공간' 이해 Chemical space

    The space of all possible molecules — ~10⁶⁰ drug-like, more than the atoms in the solar system. Each descriptor is a dimension. Reduce with PCA/t-SNE/UMAP to see the region your data occupies, and learn to distrust predictions outside it (applicability domain).

    61
  • 2.2

    빅데이터 활용과 웹 스크래핑 기술 소개 Data sources & scraping

    PubChem, ChEMBL, ZINC, the PDB. Likely requests + BeautifulSoup plus PUG-REST.

    64

In practice — prefer the official REST APIs over HTML scraping. PubChem and ChEMBL both publish one, they are rate-limit friendly, and they survive page redesigns. If the chapter demonstrates HTML scraping, treat it as pedagogy.

  • 3.6

    Nested if/else splits. Fully interpretable, individually weak.

  • 3.8

    Maximum-margin boundary; the kernel trick handles nonlinearity. Strong on small datasets — which describes most chemistry datasets.

    K-최근접 이웃 KNN

    Predict from the k most similar molecules. Note this is §1.8 turned into an algorithm — with Tanimoto as the metric, KNN is read-across from analogs, exactly what a medicinal chemist does by hand.

    XGBOOST Gradient boosting

    Trees built sequentially, each correcting the last one's errors. Usually the top performer on tabular molecular data — and the number Ch 4 has to beat.

    분류 모델의 평가와 성능 개선 Evaluation

양자화학의 기초

Foundations of quantum chemistrypp. 255–33410 sections

Eighty pages and a genuine change of register. Chapters 1–4 are data-driven; this is first-principles physics with no machine learning in it at all — solving the Schrödinger equation numerically in Python. The sections follow the canonical order of increasing difficulty every physical-chemistry course uses.

  • 5.1

    양자화학의 기본 이론 Postulates

    ψ contains everything knowable; |ψ|² is a probability density; observables are operators; Ĥψ = Eψ is an eigenvalue problem.

    255
  • 5.2

    파동 함수의 개념과 특성 The wavefunction

    Normalization, orthogonality, expectation values.

    257
  • 5.3

    무한히 깊은 퍼텐셜 상자 Particle in a box

    The simplest exactly-solvable system. E ∝ n² falls out of the boundary conditions alone — quantization stops being an assertion and becomes a result. Numerically: discretize the axis, build Ĥ as a matrix, call an eigensolver. Twenty lines of NumPy.

    266
  • 5.4

    2차원 퍼텐셜 상자 2D box

    Introduces degeneracy: (n=1,2) and (2,1) share an energy. Degeneracy is what later explains orbital shells and selection rules.

    271
  • 5.5

    조화 진동 운동 Harmonic oscillator

    The model of a vibrating bond and the basis of IR spectroscopy. Zero-point energy appears: a bond never stops vibrating, even at 0 K.

    276
  • 5.6

    양자 터널링 효과와 유한 퍼텐셜 Tunneling

    Nonzero probability of crossing a barrier it cannot climb. Chemically real — proton tunneling speeds enzyme reactions and produces kinetic isotope effects.

    286
  • 5.7

    양자 역학의 회전운동과 에너지 준위 Rotation

    Rigid rotor, angular momentum, spherical harmonics. Underlies rotational and microwave spectroscopy.

    293
  • 5.8

    수소 원자의 양자 상태와 파동 함수 The hydrogen atom

    The last system solvable exactly by hand. n, ℓ, m emerge from the mathematics, and the shapes they produce are the s/p/d orbitals from general chemistry. The payoff of 5.3–5.7.

    297

Terms of art

Vocabulary the book assumes
SMILES §1.1
A molecule as a string. Letters are atoms, lowercase means aromatic, parentheses branch, digits open and close rings. CC(=O)Oc1ccccc1C(=O)O is aspirin.
InChIKey §1.1
A fixed-length hash of the standardized identifier. One per molecule regardless of how it was drawn — the right database key.
Descriptor §1.2
A computed number summarizing a molecule: MW, LogP, TPSA. Physically interpretable, unlike a fingerprint bit.
ECFP / Morgan §1.7
Substructures within radius r of each atom, hashed into a 2048-bit vector. Turns a variable-size graph into fixed-size model input.
Tanimoto §1.8
Shared bits ÷ total bits set — the field's default similarity. 1.0 identical, >0.7 conventionally "similar".
Activity cliff §1.8
Two near-identical molecules with wildly different activity. Where the similar-property principle — and any model built on it — breaks.
Chemical space §2.1
The abstract space of all possible molecules, ~10⁶⁰ drug-like. Each descriptor is an axis; each molecule a point.
Applicability domain §2.1
The region where a model has seen neighbors, and therefore the only region where its predictions mean anything.
Scaffold split not in book
Splitting train/test by Murcko scaffold rather than at random, so analogs cannot appear on both sides. The single most important habit to bring from outside.
Class imbalance §3.13
Real bioactivity sets are 1–5% active. Accuracy is meaningless there; use ROC-AUC or precision–recall.
Zero-point energy §5.5
The residual vibration a bond keeps at absolute zero. A direct consequence of quantization, not a measurement artifact.
Born–Oppenheimer §5.9
Nuclei are thousands of times heavier than electrons, so freeze them and solve for the electrons alone. The approximation every practical method rests on.
Variational principle §5.9
Any trial wavefunction gives an energy at or above the true ground state — so lower is strictly better, which makes optimization well posed.
Basis set §5.9
The set of functions molecular orbitals are built from. 6-31G* names one; bigger is more accurate and far more expensive.
HOMO–LUMO gap §6.3
The energy between the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied orbital. Correlates with reactivity, color, and conductivity — and makes an excellent ML feature.
Geometry optimization §6.3
Search for the lowest-energy 3D arrangement of nuclei. Always follow it with a frequency job: no imaginary frequencies means you found a real minimum.

Before you start

Four things worth knowing
  • Splitting

    Use scaffold splits, not random splits. A random split puts near-identical analogs in both train and test, inflating every score in Chapters 3 and 4 by a wide margin. RDKit ships MurckoScaffold. This is the one correction to apply throughout.

  • Software

    Substitute PySCF for Gaussian. Free, open source, installs in Colab with one line. Everything Chapter 6 teaches transfers, and you keep your money.

  • Order

    The two arcs are independent. Chapters 5–6 assume nothing from 1–4. Start wherever your interest is, and read 부록 1 first if the Python is new.

  • Baseline

    Benchmark Chapter 4 against Chapter 3. On a few thousand molecules with fingerprint features, XGBoost frequently beats a neural network. A deep learning chapter that does not report the gradient-boosting number has not told you whether it won.
